The Sommelier's Secret

Water Chemistry & Mineral Balance

A cup of tea is 99% water. Understanding your water profile is the difference between a dull brew and a radiant golden liquor.

pH

Neutral pH (6.8 - 7.2)

Hard alkaline water flattens high-grown briskness and clouds the liquor. Slightly soft, filtered spring water preserves sparkling amber hues.

TDS

Total Dissolved Solids (<100 ppm)

High mineral content blocks the extraction of delicate aroma esters. Aim for total dissolved solids between 30 and 80 ppm.

O₂

Freshly Drawn Cold Water

Never re-boil standing kettle water. Re-boiling depletes dissolved oxygen, making Ceylon black tea taste flat and lifeless.

Modern Refreshment

Cold Brew High Mountain Ceylon

Cold extraction in chilled spring water extracts maximum polyphenols, floral sweetness, and natural fruity brightness without releasing astringent tannins.

1

Add 10g of whole-leaf Ceylon OP1 or Silver Tips to a clean 1-Liter glass carafe.

2

Fill with chilled pure mineral water. Add a sliver of organic lemon peel or fresh mint sprig if desired.

3

Refrigerate for 8 to 12 hours. Strain into chilled wine glasses over crystal ice spheres.
